# Ranel — Business Architecture Lock

**Status:** LOCKED  
**Purpose:** Canonical business structure for products, systems, commerce, and demand.

## 1. Operating thesis

Ranel wins by making small/local businesses easier to run, then packaging what works into repeatable commercial assets.

The sequence is:

**Observe → understand → solve narrowly → sell/test → deliver → measure → standardize → automate → distribute wider**

Do not reverse the sequence by building a large platform before the underlying workflow is evidenced.

## 2. Commercial layers

### Layer A — Kits

Kits convert knowledge into usable assets with low marginal delivery cost.

Examples:
- operations/SOP kits
- pricing/margin worksheets
- customer-return workflows
- review checklists
- business trackers
- starter playbooks

Primary business role:
- entry product;
- demand-learning instrument;
- reusable IP;
- low-cost distribution unit.

### Layer B — Systems

Systems convert repeated workflows into durable operating capability.

Examples:
- customer/visit tracking
- reporting
- retention
- inquiry/booking
- loyalty
- reminders
- automation
- lightweight operator workspaces

Primary business role:
- increase recurring value;
- reduce repetitive work;
- create stronger retention;
- create recurring revenue only when recurring value is real.

### Layer C — Supply

Supply turns verified operator needs into commerce/distribution.

Examples:
- recommended tools;
- business supplies;
- partner products;
- affiliate/referral offers;
- distribution relationships;
- private-label supply later.

Primary business role:
- transaction expansion;
- convenience;
- distribution economics;
- additional monetization around proven workflows.

## 3. Vertical strategy

Start with **barber businesses**.

The first vertical is a wedge, not the permanent limit of the brand.

Potential future verticals may include:
- café;
- laundry;
- salon;
- other local service/UMKM businesses.

Expansion requires evidence that:
1. the problem repeats;
2. the buyer is understandable;
3. the offer is deliverable;
4. the economics are workable;
5. the workflow transfers without pretending every vertical is identical.

## 4. Product ladder

The product ladder is not automatically “cheap → expensive.”

It is:

**problem clarity → useful asset → recurring workflow → integrated system → adjacent commerce**

A customer should move deeper only when actual needs justify it.

## 5. Demand-first rule

Ranel does not use feature count as a proxy for business progress.

Valid progress signals include:
- repeated problem evidence;
- qualified conversations;
- paid pilots;
- actual use;
- repeat use;
- reduced delivery/support burden;
- repeat purchase;
- healthy contribution margin;
- clear expansion demand.

Likes, compliments, traffic, and a completed UI are not substitutes for those signals.

## 6. Business economics

Revenue can come from:
- one-time digital products;
- implementation/setup;
- recurring software/service where ongoing value exists;
- onboarding/training;
- affiliate/distribution margin;
- later supply/private-label economics.

Cost discipline:
- avoid unnecessary paid APIs;
- avoid inventory before demand is understood;
- avoid large custom builds before repeatability;
- track payment fees, refunds, taxes, hosting, fulfillment, support time, and founder time.

## 7. Current barber wedge

The current public Phase 2 catalog contains:

### Ranel Barber Starter
Daily operating foundation with SOPs, menu/pricing structure, opening/closing routines and basic permission-based repeat-customer workflow.

### Ranel Barber Growth
Starter plus structured records, permission-based retention/promo workflow, service/revenue recap, operational review and small growth actions.

### Ranel Barber System
Requirements/flow/prioritization concept for a future digital system; it is not currently software.

These are current pilot definitions, not proof that the market has selected them.

## 8. Business gate model

**Gate 0 — Demand evidence**
A repeated problem is observed and documented.

**Gate 1 — Offer test**
A narrow offer is defined and put in front of qualified operators.

**Gate 2 — Paid pilot**
Payment/deposit becomes evidence of willingness to pay.

**Gate 3 — Delivery economics**
The offer can be delivered with acceptable time, support, and margin.

**Gate 4 — Standardization**
The work can be repeatably delivered with documented SOP/materials.

**Gate 5 — Automation**
Only the repeated/manual bottleneck is automated.

**Gate 6 — Scale/distribution**
Distribution widens after the offer and delivery model are stable.

## 9. Non-negotiable business rules

Never:
- fabricate demand;
- fabricate testimonials;
- claim guaranteed growth;
- charge for an unavailable feature;
- lock in unsupported pricing;
- collect unnecessary customer data;
- expand verticals simply because the architecture can support them;
- build automation to solve an unobserved problem.

## 10. Strategic result

Ranel is designed to compound **knowledge → products → systems → commerce → data → intelligence → better products**.

That compounding loop is the business architecture, not any single app feature.
