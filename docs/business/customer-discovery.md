# Customer Discovery and Demand Validation

## Objective
Find one recurring, costly, reachable problem for a specific barber-business segment and establish whether operators will pay for a practical solution.

## Interview plan
Conduct 10–15 short interviews before a substantial product build. Include operators with different customer volumes and levels of formality.

Ask about recent behavior:
1. Walk me through a normal busy day.
2. What admin or customer task gets forgotten most often?
3. Tell me about the last time that caused a problem.
4. How do you handle it today?
5. What tools or workarounds are involved?
6. How much time or money does the workaround consume?
7. Have you paid to solve this before? What happened?
8. Who decides and pays for a new tool?
9. What would make you refuse to use a solution?
10. Would you pay to test a small solution this month? What must it include?

Do not lead with “Would you use my app?” and do not pitch before understanding the workflow.

## Evidence log
Record date, business type, problem in the customer's words, last concrete occurrence, frequency, consequence, workaround, buyer, willingness-to-pay signal, follow-up permission, and next action. Do not store unnecessary personal data. Obtain consent before recording or quoting.

## Demand signal hierarchy
Likes and general encouragement are weak. Repeated problem stories are stronger. Requests for a demo, willingness to share workflow for a pilot, deposits, paid pilots, repeat use, and renewal are progressively stronger.

## Decision gate
Proceed to a paid pilot when at least three independent operators describe substantially the same problem and at least two agree to a concrete next step. These are working thresholds, not statistical proof. Revise if evidence contradicts the hypothesis.
